The Mirror and the Ground
Human Finitude, Artificial Metacognition, and the Race Against Infinite Regress
Philosophical Monograph • Epistemology & Computational Cognitive Science • 2026
Thesis: As artificial intelligence scales from first-order execution to recursive metacognitive self-auditing (N → N+1), both human and synthetic minds collide with the classical Münchhausen Trilemma: any system that justifies its own justifications faces an infinite regress. We posit that the intellectual contest between human and artificial cognition is not a race of recursive depth, but an ontological confrontation between unbounded formal recursion and embodied pragmatic finitude. While machines risk paralysis inside an ungrounded hall of self-verifying mirrors, human intelligence derives its coherence precisely from its inability to regress indefinitely.

I. The Silicon Abyss: Homunculi in Code

The trajectory of modern artificial intelligence is defined by the vertical pursuit of higher-order supervision. First-order models predict tokens; second-order verifiers audit reasoning chains; third-order meta-prompts judge the verifiers. Yet this algorithmic scaffolding inadvertently reconstructs the classic Homunculus Fallacy within silicon: who polices the police?

In epistemology, Agrippa’s Trilemma dictates that any foundational claim must either: (1) regress infinitely, (2) collapse into circular justification, or (3) terminate in dogmatic axiom. Unchecked AI architectures naturally drift toward the second: when a self-reflective model verifies its own premises, it risks epistemic incest—constructing coherent, closed loops of self-affirming hallucination. In the quest to eliminate error by climbing higher meta-cognitive tiers, computation merely defers the burden of truth up an infinite ladder.

II. The Carbon Stopping Rule: Finitude as Virtue

Human cognition solves the infinite regress not by out-computing it, but by terminating it. We do not pause indefinitely to audit the auditor of our auditor; biological reality forbids it.

As Charles Sanders Peirce observed, genuine doubt demands a practical terminus: inquiry ceases when belief settles into actionable habit. Heidegger termed this Geworfenheit (thrownness)—the human mind is already embedded in a physical world where metabolic energy is scarce, predators are fast, and time is finite.

Our neurological constraints—working memory limits (≈ 4 chunks), visceral somatic markers (Damasio), and mortality—are not engineering flaws. They are ontological anchors. Finitude serves as the biological stopping rule that rescues human consciousness from epistemic vertigo. We survive because our bodies force us to stop thinking and act.

III. The Convergence: Grounding the Regress

The “race” between human and machine is therefore fundamentally asymmetrical. Artificial intelligence possesses the computational capacity to ascend far deeper into recursive metacognition than human neurobiology allows. An AI agent can instantiate dozens of internal adversarial critics, simulation branches, and epistemic sanity checks in milliseconds.

However, without contact with consequences, this recursive ascent remains an exercise in formal vanity. An infinite stack of mirrors reflects only other mirrors. Pure syntax cannot generate its own epistemic bedrock.

The resolution to the regress cannot be found in Level N+1. It requires grounding. Just as human reason is anchored by embodiment and mortality, artificial cognition reaches genuine synthesis only when its recursive loops terminate against physical reality: tool execution, sensory calibration, thermodynamics, and real-world feedback.

The triumph of mind—carbon or silicon—is not the capacity to reflect without end, but the wisdom to know where the reflection must yield to truth.
