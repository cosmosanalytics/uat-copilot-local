Financial World Model (Simplified)

A small local LLM orchestrates 9 tool calls — one per stage of a single applicant's standard life insurance / annuity journey (Application → Health Questionnaire → Records Check → Medical Exam → Underwriter Review → Rating & Premium Pricing → Policy Issuance → Premium Servicing / Accumulation → then either Claims Processing or Annuitization, depending on the product family). Each tool is a pure, deterministic function modeling the real math and gating rules of that stage (a debit-based risk-classification engine, age/risk-adjusted premium pricing, contestability-period claim investigation, surrender-charge schedules); the model's only job is deciding which to call, in what order. Run stages manually to study the logic, or let the agent orchestrate the whole chain. The stage sequence and product lines are modeled loosely on Lincoln Financial Group's core business (life insurance and annuities — see footer), simplified into a single illustrative pipeline — not a representation of Lincoln's or any specific insurer's actual underwriting manual, rate tables, or contract terms.

Product & application
A medical exam is triggered automatically if the life face amount is ≥ $500,000 or any medical condition is disclosed (a simplified version of real exam-requirement rules). Annuities skip medical underwriting risk-pricing in practice, but this pipeline still runs them through the same review stages for illustration — the risk tier just won't affect an annuity's premium math the way it does for life.
Accumulation
Outcome — Claims (life) or Annuitization (annuity)
Claims within the 2-year contestability window are denied if an undisclosed item was found during Records Check (material misrepresentation) — a simplified version of real contestability-clause investigations. Annuity surrender charges follow a declining 10-year schedule (9% in year 1 down to 0% by year 10+).
