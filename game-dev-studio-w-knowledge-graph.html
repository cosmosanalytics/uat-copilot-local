A team of AI agents with different jobs designs, builds, playtests and ships a small video game (WebLLM agents, WebGPU, one HTML file)

A game dev team w knowledge graph: from pitch to playable game

Give the studio a one-line pitch. A producer sets the concept, three game designers with different lenses tune three different games, an artist picks the sprites and backdrop, and a composer picks the music. Plain code builds each game in a real engine. Then bot players at three skill levels play every build many times. A critic that sees only the playtest numbers finds what is broken, QA fixes it, and a curator ranks the builds. You play them, pick one, and download it as a standalone HTML file.

  1. Producer: Turns the pitch into a title, genre, theme and palette.
  2. Three game designers: Arcade purist, cozy collector and survival hardcore, so the difficulty differs.
  3. Artist and composer: Sprites, backdrop and a generated chiptune score for each build.
  4. Playtest bots: Code players (novice, average, expert) measure win rate, pacing and tension.
  5. Critic, QA and curator: The critic sees only numbers, QA patches the game rules, the curator ranks. You pick.

Play area

Build a game, then press Play on one of the cards. Controls: arrow keys or WASD to move, Space or Up to jump. On a phone, drag to move or tap to jump.

Honest label: the agents are a small on-device chat model, so some replies cannot be read, and the page then falls back to simple rules and says so. The model fills in the settings of a fixed engine (a dodge-and-collect game and an endless runner). It does not write new game code, so every game is playable and every playtest is real. The bot players are simple scripts, so a win rate says how hard the game is, not whether it is fun. Your own play is the real test. Model weights download from huggingface.co and are cached in your browser. Nothing you type leaves your device.
