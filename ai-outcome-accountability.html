iWhat this demonstrates

Most "AI accountability" demos stop at process metadata — who approved what, when. This one goes one step further: it closes the loop back to real-world outcomes. A local model (WebLLM) makes a policy recommendation by answering the same question several times and voting — its confidence is measured, not self-reported. Low-confidence or high-risk cases require a named human reviewer before they count as final. Later, you record what actually happened, and the console computes real accuracy — overall, by confidence level, and by reviewer — and flags it if recent accuracy is slipping. That closing step (decision → outcome → accountability) is what most AI tools skip.
