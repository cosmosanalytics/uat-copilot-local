An orchestra team: from request to a performed classic

Ask for a piece and a size of ensemble. A conductor picks the piece and the ensemble. Three maestros with different lenses plan three interpretations, and a concertmaster picks the lead instrument and the bowing. Plain code writes out every part from the score, synthesizes the orchestra in your browser and renders each interpretation offline. A sound critic that hears only measurements finds what is wrong with the balance, the loudness or the dynamics. The team fixes it, and a curator ranks the takes. You listen, pick, and can download a WAV.

  1. Conductor: Chooses the piece (Ode to Joy, Canon in D, Für Elise or Minuet in G) and a string quartet, chamber group or full orchestra.
  2. Three maestros: Classical, Romantic and Period lenses set tempo, expression, rubato, warmth and reverb.
  3. Concertmaster: Picks the lead instrument and the articulation for each take.
  4. Players (code): Violins, viola, cello, bass, flute, oboe, clarinet, horn, trumpet, harp, piano, harpsichord and timpani, synthesized with Web Audio.
  5. Sound critic, fixes and curator: Melody balance, headroom, dynamic range and brightness are measured. A critic that sees only numbers, then a ranking. You make the final pick.

The stage

Build a performance, then press Listen on one of the takes.

Honest label: the agents are a small on-device chat model, so some replies cannot be read, and the page then falls back to simple rules and says so. The music is real score data for four public domain classics, arranged here for the ensemble (the melody is from the score, and the accompaniment is generated from the chords). The sounds are simple synthesized instruments, not recordings, so they sound like a stylized orchestra and not a real hall. The measurements check balance, headroom, dynamic range and brightness. They do not measure beauty, so your own ears are the real test. The WebLLM model downloads from huggingface.co and is cached in your browser. Nothing you type leaves your device.
