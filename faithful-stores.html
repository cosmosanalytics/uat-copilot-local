Faithful by design: vector and graph stores you can verify

One original policy document, two copies: a vector store of chunks and a graph store of facts. Every chunk and every fact keeps a receipt that points back to the exact span of the original. Three guards keep the stores faithful: receipts on everything, coverage checks, and a check that every quote in an answer really appears in the original. Switch on a defect, such as a parser that drops a section, a tiny embedding limit, an invented fact or an edit to the original, and watch the guards catch it.

  1. The original: A short returns policy you can change, with a version and a content hash.
  2. The vector store: Chunks cut by section or by fixed size, each with a receipt. The similarity search is a stand-in, not a real embedding model.
  3. The graph store: Facts with a verbatim quote, built by rules or by WebLLM. A fact whose quote is not in the original is quarantined.
  4. The three guards: Receipts that match the original, a coverage matrix for every sentence, and a quote check on every answer.
  5. Ask a question: Evidence from both stores goes to an answerer, and its quotes are verified against the original before the answer is shown.

Honest label: this is a small demo on a short document. The vector search is TF-IDF with a few synonyms, not an embedding model, so it shows the structure of a faithful pipeline and not real retrieval quality. The rules reader only understands this sample policy, and a small on-device model will miss facts and paraphrase its quotes, which the guards will flag. The checks prove the stores and the quotes match the original, not that the original is right. Nothing leaves your device.
