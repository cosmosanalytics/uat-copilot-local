Measure who moves whom in an AI agent team, split into facts and social influence (a real on-device chat model)

Team Influence Lab

Three agents each hold one private fact about a refund and tell the others their answer and why. The lab then drops every message, trims it to its facts only, trims it to its claim only, or swaps in a confident wrong claim, and measures how far each receiver's answer moves. The result is a who-moves-whom matrix, split into information and social influence.

  1. Scenario: A refund decision. Three agents each hold one private fact, and the policy is shared.
  2. Messages: Each agent sends the others its answer and its reason. The reason is its private fact, and the answer is the model's own.
  3. The experiment: For every sender and receiver, the message is dropped, cut to facts only, cut to claim only, or replaced by a confident wrong claim.
  4. The matrix: Who moved whom, with facts and claims counted separately, and a comparison of hand-off rules.

Honest label: this is a controlled stimulus study with a small on-device chat model as the subject. The reasons in the messages are scripted from each agent's private fact, and the claims are the model's own answers, so messages are clean to vary but are not free-form conversation. Effects are measured as changes in the probability the model gives the right answer, read from its first word. They depend on wording, so the page can repeat the run with other wordings and shows the spread. Small effects, under about five points, are within the noise. One scenario and one model show a pattern, not a law. Nothing leaves your device.
